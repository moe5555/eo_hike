## Incident: 67 GB of junk in `.git` (2026-11-01)

My fault in the part that matters, and the cause is only partly known.

- I rendered the two films (451 MB and 348 MB) into `analysis/h0111/films/`, which git does not ignore.
- From 16:31:46 to about 19:56, something tried to store the 451 MB film in git about every 5 seconds. Each attempt died after about 4 seconds and left about 150 MB behind: 489 `tmp_obj_*` files in `.git/objects/3b`, 67 GB in all.
- The disk reached zero free by about 19:08. 14 walks failed with "No space left on device", and the machine was restarted around 19:58 after the process was killed. My guard only checked free disk between walks, so it could not stop this.
- My first diagnosis blamed the Claude app's VM image. That was wrong: my audit had skipped `.git` and filtered out files over 200 MB. I corrected it.
- **What made the attempts is not identified.** The closest candidate is a rate-limit checkpoint routine in the Claude Code binary, which hashes untracked files with a timeout of about that length. But it should also write `.claude/RESUME.md`, which does not exist, and a 30-second test with a 300 MB file reproduced nothing. Treat this as unproven.
- The fix does not depend on the cause: large outputs now go under `runs/`, which git ignores. `scripts/hikes_0111.sh` runs a watchdog every 30 seconds that stops the whole batch if free disk falls under 10 GB or `.git/objects` grows by over 1 GB. `scripts/hikes_0111.sh stop` ends it cleanly (tested, no processes left).
- Afterwards: the tile cache's metadata (3,451 files) and the 6,496 most recent tiles were checked and are intact. 29 small harmless `.part` files remain in the cache. `.git` stayed at 111 MB through the restart, the film re-render and the whole batch, with no temporary objects.
